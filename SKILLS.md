Walking through Layla's journey 

Headshot of Layla
Layla

When Layla was transitioning between jobs, she was concerned about finding a new role. She decided to talk to a coworker who was going through a similar situation.

Play the video below to listen to Layla's conversation with her coworker.



Video Transcript
Coworker: Hey Layla! How are you doing? 



Layla: Hey Nate! Not great to be honest. This round of layoffs is making me nervous. 



Coworker:  I can relate- it is a stressful time. Are you looking at other jobs?



Layla: I am trying but I haven’t had much luck. I need to find a different job where I won’t be as replaceable. All my experience has been in customer service, unfortunately! I have no idea what employers are even looking for.



Coworker:  The last time I was shifting careers, my former manager recommended that I proactively sign up for courses and certifications to learn different skillsets. 



Layla: That is interesting! What kind of skills did you learn?



Coworker: Everything ranging from thinking critically to structuring my communication! These skills are crucial to any career and are typically what most employers are looking for. And while you have to learn other skills, which can certainly be challenging sometimes, keeping your successes in mind can help you through the tough times. 



Layla: This is helpful! I did see an opening for a computer user support specialist that would allow me to work remotely. But I am hesitant to apply.



Coworker:  Why is that?



Layla: To be honest, I feel overwhelmed and slightly resentful towards technology. It seems like it’s always changing and I can never keep up! 



Coworker:  Learning how you can work and think in new ways with data and technology will not only remove the barriers between you and technology, but it will put you in a great place to apply for this job. Of course, you’ll need to continue expanding your digital skills, but a good foundation will get you started to keep learning...

How did Layla get where she is today? 


Changing her mindset to focus on what she can do well and what she would need to learn helped Layla gain the confidence to try something new. She had to become more resilient, bouncing back from obstacles and being comfortable with risks.

Learning digital skills and agile ways of working also helped her feel more in control and in a more comfortable space where she can make technology work for her. 

Layla used the skills you’ll learn in the Adaptability & Resilience and My Digital Toolkit courses. How can these courses help you on your professional journey?

Walking through Victoria's journey  


Victoria

Once Victoria settled into her new job as a program coordinator, she was  approached with working on a new project. 

Play the video below to listen to Victoria's conversation with her manager. 



Video Transcript
Manager: Hey Victoria! I wanted to give you a rundown of your project for the next couple of weeks before our team meeting. We’re releasing the new app in four weeks and need to make sure everyone is on the same page. Do you mind drafting up a workplan for the next month and getting feedback from the rest of the team?



Victoria: Uhhhh… sure!



Victoria: I have no idea how to start this workplan. I’m used to planning and execution, not brainstorming different solutions and getting input from different individuals. And this problem has so many moving pieces! I should ask my manager for help.



Manager: Hey Victoria, Everything okay?



Victoria: I was just wondering if you could help me get started on this workplan. I’m having trouble breaking down all the pieces of work that need to get done.



Manager: No worries at all! There are a few different things we want to evaluate when we are trying to solve a problem:



1.    What are we trying to solve exactly?

2.   What does the end goal look like? And how do we get there?

3.   How much time...?

How did Victoria get where she is today? 

Learning how to problem solve not only taught Victoria how to be more independent but also helped her think creatively and get input from other people, bringing out the team leader they always were! 

Victoria used the skills you’ll learn in the Problem Solving course. How can this course help you on your professional journey?

Walking through Gerri's journey 

Headshot of Gerri

Gerri

After Gerri starts working as an advertising sales agent for Bubbles & Co., he regularly connected with his mentor, Linda, to talk about anything on his mind.

Play the video below to listen to Gerri's conversation with his mentor, Linda.



Video Transcript
Linda: Hi Gerri! How are you fitting in your new role?



Gerri: Hi Linda! The work has been very exciting! Unfortunately…, I am having difficulty connecting with my coworkers and new manager.



Linda: Oh no! What’s the problem? 



Gerri: I don’t know how I’m doing in my new position, and I’m nervous asking my manager for feedback. 



Linda: I gotcha! You should really think of it as an opportunity to hear about what you’re doing well. Ask for both positive and constructive feedback from him. Why are you having a difficult time connecting with your coworkers?



Gerri: I don’t feel like I have much to connect on. Everyone’s a college grad, they’ve done internships at cool places…



Linda: I get how that can be uncomfortable, but they’d be really interested to hear a different kind of story to theirs. Opening up will help you make stronger connections here, and I’m sure your colleagues will surprise you, too. 



Linda: Is there anything else that is bothering you?



Gerri: Yes…I am wondering why my manager did not respond to one of my messages. I was trying to be more open to him about an idea but he didn’t even respond!



Linda: Do you mind if I look over the message? I can help and see what is going on.



Gerri: Sure!



Linda: I can see what might have happened. It’s kinda hard to follow since it’s so long. Your manager probably needs some time to figure out what the message and next steps are……



Gerri: Well, I really wanted to talk about the information I got from Materials & Co., our supplier. They were telling me about all the benefits with Stretch Aluminum, like the price and that it is really thin….



Linda: That’s the problem, I hear your excitement, but not the actual message. Here’s how I’d restructure the message so that if your manager and I would be able to take action on this really great idea.



Gerri: This is great feedback! I really appreciate it! 



Linda: You have great ideas, let’s make sure they’re polished so that you shine here!

How did Gerri get where he is today?

With feedback from Linda, Gerri learned to better communicate to any audience both verbally and on paper with a structured and impact-driven approach. He also built strong relationships at work and became good friends with his colleagues, eventually starting a business with one.

Gerri used the skills you’ll learn in the Communicating for Impact and Relationships & Well-Being courses. How can these courses help you on your professional journey?
